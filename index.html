The $10 Million Appalachian Highlands Supply Chain Challenge

You are the COO of a growing regional manufacturer. Five problems hit in one quarter: a supplier failure, a healthcare demand surge, an AI investment decision, a quality breakdown, and a risky AI recommendation. Can you protect the business?

5 decisions • 8–10 minutes

Your fictional company: RidgeLine Systems

RidgeLine Systems is a fictional B2B advanced-manufacturing company headquartered in the Appalachian Highlands.

It assembles connected monitoring units, control enclosures, and high-value replacement components used by hospitals, industrial OEMs, and commercial facilities. The business uses a hybrid make-to-stock / make-to-order model: common components are stocked, while higher-value finished configurations are assembled to customer demand. RidgeLine buys electronics globally, sources fabricated metal and plastics regionally, and operates one final-assembly plant and one distribution center in Northeast Tennessee.

You are the Chief Operating Officer. Procurement, planning, manufacturing, logistics, customer service, and digital transformation all report into your executive team. Your job in this simulation is to protect enterprise value—not simply minimize purchase price.

Your goal is not simply to maximize short-term profit. Strong business decisions must balance profit, customer impact, resilience, innovation, and limited discretionary cash. There is no fixed “best answer” in each round. Earlier choices change later outcomes, and a strategy that is optimal after one path may be poor after another. The final score also penalizes imbalanced portfolios—for example, excellent resilience purchased at the expense of profitability or customer service.

Why the game’s decision logic is defensible

Supply-chain practice behind the scenarios

The RidgeLine company and numerical outcomes are fictional, but the strategic logic was checked against established supply-chain, quality, and AI-management guidance.

Structural resilience: dual sourcing and prequalified alternatives
McKinsey’s supply-chain resilience work identifies dual sourcing, regionalization, visibility, and inventory positioning as common structural responses to disruption.
McKinsey source ↗
AI: prioritize feasible use cases with measurable business value
Gartner’s 2025 supply-chain guidance argues that “AI for the sake of AI” does not create value and that leaders should evaluate use cases on both feasibility and business value, including data readiness.
Gartner source ↗
Process improvement: diagnose before implementing the countermeasure
ASQ’s DMAIC framework explicitly moves from Define and Measure to Analyze root causes before Improve and Control, supporting the game’s preference for diagnosis over immediate capital expansion.
ASQ source ↗
Human + AI: operational context and oversight matter
NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework stresses human roles, oversight, context of use, known limitations, and the risk that mathematical models remove important context from complex decisions.
NIST source ↗
