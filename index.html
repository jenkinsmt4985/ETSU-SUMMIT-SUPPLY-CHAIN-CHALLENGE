The $10 Million Appalachian Highlands Supply Chain Challenge

You are the COO of a growing regional manufacturer. Five problems hit in one quarter: a supplier failure, a healthcare demand surge, an AI investment decision, a quality breakdown, and a risky AI recommendation. Can you protect the business?

5 decisions • 8–10 minutes

Your company: RidgeLine Systems

RidgeLine Systems is an advanced-manufacturing company headquartered in the Appalachian Highlands.

The company assembles connected monitoring units, control enclosures, and high-value replacement components for hospitals, industrial OEMs, and commercial facilities. Common components are stocked, while higher-value finished products are configured to customer demand. RidgeLine buys electronics globally, sources fabricated metal and plastics regionally, and operates a final-assembly plant and distribution center in Northeast Tennessee.

You are the Chief Operating Officer. Procurement, planning, manufacturing, logistics, customer service, and digital transformation all fall under your team. Over the next five decisions, you will have to keep customers supplied, protect margins, manage risk, and decide where to spend limited cash.
Annual revenue$48M
Quarter revenue at risk$10M
Suppliers74
Employees185

There is more than one reasonable way to handle each problem. Keep an eye on profit, customer service, resilience, innovation, and cash. What you spend or sacrifice early in the game will affect the choices available to you later.

How the scenarios were built

Supply-chain thinking behind the scenarios

RidgeLine and its numbers were created for the challenge. The decisions themselves draw on established practices in supply-chain resilience, AI adoption, process improvement, and human oversight.

Structural resilience: dual sourcing and prequalified alternatives
McKinsey’s supply-chain resilience work identifies dual sourcing, regionalization, visibility, and inventory positioning as common structural responses to disruption.
McKinsey source ↗
AI: prioritize feasible use cases with measurable business value
Gartner’s 2025 supply-chain guidance argues that “AI for the sake of AI” does not create value and that leaders should evaluate use cases on both feasibility and business value, including data readiness.
Gartner source ↗
Process improvement: diagnose before implementing the countermeasure
ASQ’s DMAIC framework explicitly moves from Define and Measure to Analyze root causes before Improve and Control, supporting the game’s preference for diagnosis over immediate capital expansion.
ASQ source ↗
Human + AI: operational context and oversight matter
NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework stresses human roles, oversight, context of use, known limitations, and the risk that mathematical models remove important context from complex decisions.
NIST source ↗
